Flagship Project

Giving Feedback in Cross-Cultural Teams

A scenario-based microlearning module helping multilingual employees decode common American workplace feedback patterns.

The challenge

A workforce with many multilingual employees kept losing signal in manager feedback — indirect phrasing, the "feedback sandwich," mismatched urgency in written notes. Not a language-fluency problem. A pattern-recognition gapThis framing — a performance gap, not a knowledge gap — is what separates ID thinking from just "make a course.".

What I built

A 10–15 minute branching-scenario module: a feedback conversation going wrong, five decision-point scenarios drawn from real classroom-observed patterns, and a closing rewrite exercise.

ADDIE-framed, SAM-paced Rise 360 Branching scenarios AI-assisted drafting

Where AI fit — and where it didn't

I used Claude to generate a first draft of five scenario dialogues. Then I rewrote each one against seven years of watching these exact misunderstandings happen — correcting tone, cutting stereotype risk, and grounding the "coaching notes" in real classroom patterns rather than generic advice. The judgment was mineThis is the line reviewers are specifically screening for right now — AI accelerates drafts; it doesn't replace pedagogical judgment.; AI moved the first draft faster.

Process

How I work

01

Diagnose before designing

Is this actually a training-solvable problem? I ask this before opening an authoring tool.

02

Objectives tied to behavior

Not "understand X" — "given Y, learner will do Z." Measurable, or it doesn't count.

03

Draft fast, revise by hand

AI drafts, I edit — an ADDIE skeleton with SAM-style iterative refinement inside it.

04

Plan the evaluation up front

Kirkpatrick-informed: how will I know if this actually changed behavior, not just completion rates?
